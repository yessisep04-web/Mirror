Communication Lab

Use this when your explanation feels incomplete, facts changed, or you are worried that someone thinks you are being dishonest.

1. What I know

2. What I am uncertain about

3. What changed

4. What I said earlier

5. What I know now

6. What I want the other person to understand

Clarity Formula

Fact → uncertainty → correction → current understanding → question.

Example: “What I know is ___. I was unsure about ___. Earlier I said ___. I have since learned ___. What I want to clarify is ___.”

Changing or correcting details does not by itself establish intentional deception. The useful question is whether you are trying to communicate accurately with the information you currently have.
